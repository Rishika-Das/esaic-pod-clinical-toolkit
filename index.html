ESAIC Recommendation 3.4 — Biomarkers Not Recommended:

The routine clinical use of blood or CSF biomarkers (such as S100B, tau, or inflammatory cytokines) for predicting postoperative delirium is not recommended (Low evidence, Weak recommendation). Rely on clinical risk factors.

Recommendation 5.1 & 5.2 • Low Evidence, Weak Recommendation

EEG Depth of Anesthesia & Spectral Array Console

Simulation: Over-sedation

ESAIC recommends index-based monitoring, but explicitly warns against relying exclusively on numeric indices (e.g., BIS/PSI). Clinicians must monitor raw EEG for Burst Suppression and track the Density Spectral Array (DSA) for alpha-band (8–12 Hz) power.

EEG Index (BIS) 24
Suppr. Ratio (SR%) 38%
Alpha Power (8-12Hz) Lost / Suppr.

CRITICAL: Burst Suppression Detected (Associated with Increased POD Risk)

The raw EEG demonstrates isoelectric periods and Suppression Ratio > 0%. Furthermore, alpha-band activity (8–12 Hz) is attenuated. Action: Immediately titrate down volatile or IV anesthetic concentration to eliminate suppression periods while maintaining surgical analgesia.

Recommendation 3.3 — Anesthetic Technique: Neither a specific anesthetic technique (e.g. volatile vs. Total Intravenous Anesthesia / TIVA) nor a specific surgical technique is recommended to reduce POD incidence (Low evidence, Weak recommendation). How the anesthetic is titrated (avoiding excessive depth and burst suppression) is key.
Interpretation Guide ESAIC Rec 5.2

Why Numeric Indices Alone Are Insufficient

A commercial index (e.g. BIS 45) can appear falsely "normal" during intermittent burst suppression or severe age-related neurovascular deceleration.

Target EEG State:
  • Continuous slow-delta waves with overlaid continuous alpha spindle rhythm (8–12 Hz).
  • DSA reveals a distinct, unbroken high-power band in the 8–12 Hz zone.
  • Burst Suppression Ratio = 0%.
Delirogenic EEG State:
  • Isoelectric flatline intervals interspersed with high-amplitude sharp bursts.
  • Alpha Dropout: loss of 8–12 Hz power on the DSA spectrogram.
  • Associated with postoperative neuroinflammation and prolonged delirium.
Training Proposal:

ESAIC explicitly proposes structured clinical training for anesthesiologists to interpret raw EEG traces and density spectral array patterns routinely.

Recommendation 4.3 • Moderate Evidence, Strong Recommendation

Non-Pharmacological Multicomponent Prevention Bundle

ESAIC strongly recommends a comprehensive multicomponent bundle for all patients at risk. A bundled package of non-pharmacological interventions is significantly more effective than single isolated measures.

Protocol Adherence Rate 83% Complete
1. Cognitive Reorientation & Psychological De-stressing
Domain 1
Environmental Time & Space Anchors
Ensure large clock, date calendar, and window orientation are in direct patient line of sight.
Structured Verbal Reorientation
Introduce staff names, repeat location, explain postoperative progress calmly at each contact.
Family Presence & Familiar Objects
Facilitate bedside visits by trusted relatives or personal photos to decrease acute anxiety.
2. Sensory Aid Provision (Vision & Hearing)
Domain 2
Immediate Return of Eyeglasses
Clean optical glasses provided in PACU immediately upon patient emergence.
Hearing Aids Active & Functional
Verify functioning batteries, insert hearing aids, avoid unnecessary acoustic isolation.
Dentures Returned
Promote comfort, clear communication, and early nutritional intake.
3. Sleep Architecture Promotion & Noise Reduction
Domain 3
Noise Muffling & Earplugs
Provide soft silicone earplugs; lower monitor telemetry alarm thresholds overnight.
Eye Masks & Circadian Lighting
Dim ambient ward lights between 22:00 and 06:00; bright natural daylight during morning.
Clustered Nocturnal Nursing Care
Coordinate blood draws, medication administration, and vital signs to avoid sleep fragmentation.
4. Early Catheter Removal & De-tethering
Domain 4
Indwelling Urinary Catheter Removal
Remove transurethral catheter on POD 0 or early POD 1 unless strictly indicated.
Early Discontinuation of Arterial/Venous Lines
Prompt removal of redundant central lines and invasive monitors to allow free movement.
Strict Avoidance of Physical Restraints
Restraints heighten delirium agitation and trigger catastrophic falls.
5. Hydration, Nutrition & Early Mobilization
Domain 5
Early Oral Hydration & Food Intake
Sips of water in recovery room; progress to normal diet without unnecessary NPO delays.
Bedside Mobilization within 6 Hours
Assist patient to dangling at edge of bed on day of surgery; upright chair sitting.
Assisted Ambulation by POD 1
Active walking with physical therapy or nursing team; opioid-sparing pain protocol.

Individual measures are hard to assess in isolation, but evidence demonstrates the bundled synergy dramatically reduces delirium incidence and duration.

Clinical Implementation Note

Assign dedicated nursing ownership for sensory aids (glasses/hearing aids) and catheter removal times to prevent protocol breakdowns.

ESAIC Recommendation 3.1 & Core Medical Principle ("Primum Non Nocere"):

Routine pharmacological prophylaxis against POD is NOT recommended for surgical patients in general (Low evidence, Weak recommendation). Pharmacological therapy is reserved strictly as second-line treatment when non-pharmacological interventions fail or acute safety is compromised.

Recommendation 6.1 • Very Low Evidence, Weak Recommendation

Haloperidol Treatment Protocol

Symptom-Oriented

Haloperidol should be administered at low doses, for short duration, and titrated strictly to symptom control only when non-pharmacological measures fail.

Recommended Bolus Dose: 0.125 – 0.25 mg IV
Maximum 24h Daily Limit: 3.0 mg / 24 Hours
Logged 24h Dose
0.50 mg
Strict Caution / Contraindication:

Patients with preexisting Parkinson's Disease or Dementia with Lewy Bodies (DLB): Antipsychotics must be used with extreme caution or avoided completely due to life-threatening acute parkinsonian crisis or neuroleptic malignant syndrome.

Recommendation 6.2 • Very Low Evidence, Weak Recommendation

Benzodiazepine Guidelines: Strict Ban & Exception

Explicitly NOT Recommended

Evidence of benefit for benzodiazepines in treating POD symptoms is negligible or absent. Administration often precipitates or exacerbates delirium.

BENZODIAZEPINES CONTRAINDICATED FOR STANDARD POD

Do NOT administer midazolam, lorazepam, or diazepam for hypoactive or hyperactive postoperative delirium. They worsen cognitive fragmentation and prolong ICU length of stay.

FIRST-LINE EXCEPTION: Alcohol Withdrawal Delirium

In alcohol withdrawal delirium, benzodiazepines ARE the medication of first choice! Titrate lowest effective IV bolus dose symptom-guided (e.g. CIWA-Ar protocol).

Recommendation 3.2 & 6.3 • Dexmedetomidine Evaluation Framework

Dexmedetomidine: Cardiac vs. Non-Cardiac & Safety Warnings

Risk-Benefit Analysis Mandatory

ESAIC distinguishes sharply between prophylactic vs. therapeutic dexmedetomidine, and between cardiac vs. non-cardiac surgery cohorts.

A. Prophylactic Use Rec 3.2 (Strong)

Evidence is contradictory. Not generally recommended for all patients.

Mandatory Risk-Benefit Weighing:

Anticipated benefit (older patients) must be actively balanced against major adverse effects: bradycardia and arterial hypotension.

B. Delirium Therapy Rec 6.3 (Weak)
Cardiac Surgery: Recommended
Reduces POD duration, ICU/hospital stay, and mortality.
Non-Cardiac: No Recommendation
Insufficient data; cannot recommend routine therapeutic use.
C. BfArM Warning SPICE III Alert

Rote-Hand-Brief (BfArM 15.06.2022):

Increased mortality observed after dexmedetomidine administration in critically ill ICU patients ≤ 65 years of age (particularly in non-operative patients).

Interactive Dexmedetomidine Decision Matrix:
Prophylaxis Evaluation: Non-Cardiac Older Adult

Dexmedetomidine prophylaxis is not routinely recommended. If selected, continuous monitoring of arterial BP and heart rate is mandatory to guard against severe bradycardia and hypotension.
